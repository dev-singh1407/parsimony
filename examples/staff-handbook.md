# Harlow Instruments staff handbook

This handbook is fictional. It is the dev-split collection of the long-context benchmark (corpus/longctx_docs.jsonl), laid out as a file you can attach:

    parsimony ask --file examples/staff-handbook.md

## Leeds office

Harlow Instruments opened its Leeds office in 2014 as the company's first site outside London. The office occupies two floors of the Calder Building on Wellington Street. It employs 112 people, most of them in hardware testing and customer support. The Leeds site manager is Priya Raman, who joined from the Porto office in 2021. Leeds holds the company's main calibration lab, which is booked through the internal portal at least five working days in advance. The annual travel budget for Leeds staff is £84,000. Visitors must sign in at reception on the ground floor and wear a badge at all times. Parking is limited to 20 spaces, allocated by lottery each quarter.

## Porto office

The Porto office was established in 2017 to support customers in Southern Europe. It sits in a converted warehouse near the Douro riverfront. The office employs 41 people across sales, logistics and software localisation. Its annual travel budget is €52,000, which is set in euros and not converted. The Porto team runs the company's Portuguese and Spanish help desks from 8:00 to 18:00 local time. Unlike Leeds, Porto has no calibration lab, so instruments that need recalibration are shipped to Leeds by courier. The site manager is Tomás Aguiar. A rooftop terrace is available for team events and must be reserved through facilities.

## Tallinn office

Harlow Instruments acquired the embedded-software firm Rakvere Systems in 2019, and with it an office in Tallinn. The Tallinn office opened under the Harlow name in March 2019. It employs 58 people, nearly all of them firmware engineers. Tallinn does not have a customer help desk; its engineers join support calls only when a ticket is escalated to level three. The office has a travel budget of €39,000 per year. Most hardware prototypes are designed in Tallinn and then built and tested in Leeds. Staff in Tallinn work a four-day compressed week from June to August.

## Travel policy

All Harlow Instruments employees must book work travel through the Voyant booking tool. Economy class is required for flights under six hours. Flights of six hours or longer may be booked in premium economy with approval from a site manager. Hotel costs are reimbursed up to £140 per night in the United Kingdom and €150 per night elsewhere in Europe. Meals are covered by a daily allowance of £45, or €50 outside the UK. Rail travel is preferred for any journey under four hours. Receipts must be submitted within 30 days of return. Claims submitted after 60 days are not reimbursed.

## Equipment loans

Employees may borrow test equipment for fieldwork using the loan register. Standard loans last up to 14 days. Oscilloscopes and spectrum analysers are limited to 7 days because demand is high. Each loan must be approved by the borrower's line manager. Equipment that leaves the United Kingdom requires an ATA carnet, which the logistics team in Porto arranges. Damage must be reported within 24 hours using form EQ-7. Late returns are flagged to the site manager after 3 days. The register is audited every quarter.

## Onboarding

New starters at every Harlow site complete a two-week onboarding programme. The first week covers safety training, company systems and an introduction to each product line. In the second week, new staff shadow a colleague in their own team. Firmware engineers also complete a secure coding course, which takes about 12 hours. Every new starter is assigned a buddy from a different team. Laptops are issued on the first morning and must be encrypted before connecting to the network. Probation lasts six months for all roles.
