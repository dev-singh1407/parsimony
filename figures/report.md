## Additivity shortfall

- Sum of individual reductions: **29.06%**
- Measured stacked reduction (M1+M2+M3+M5): **27.43%**
- **Additivity shortfall: 1.63 percentage points (95% CI [-0.02, +3.23])**

On **input tokens alone** — the half that becomes prefill time — the same computation gives 32.73% predicted against 29.79% measured, a shortfall of **2.94 pp**. The interaction is therefore *stronger* on the side that costs time than the combined figure suggests.

Bootstrap resamples conversations, not requests: turns within a conversation share history and are not independent observations.

Quantifying this shortfall is the primary result. No published study runs these modules in one pipeline, so the field has no evidence about whether their savings compound.

## Energy and cost equivalent

**Not measured.** No energy figure is claimed anywhere in this project, and this section deliberately reports none.

Two things would have to be true for a number here to mean anything, and neither is. First, energy is *derived* — wall clock multiplied by an assumed package power of 15 W — not metered at the socket. Second, and decisively, generation in this sweep is simulated, so wall clock is dominated by Parsimony's own middleware rather than by the model. Computed anyway, the column ranks configurations by how many modules they run and declares the full stack the most expensive, which is a true statement about middleware and a false one about energy.

What would make it real: socket-level or RAPL instrumentation against the Ollama provider, at least 30 repetitions per configuration, baseline against full stack. The harness records the per-row estimate and its coverage either way (baseline 176/88, M5 176/88, M3 176/88, ...), so the arithmetic is available to anyone who wants to redo it under those conditions.

The related quantity this project *can* defend is prefill time, which is metered by the server itself: see the latency section.

### Does each finding transfer?

| claim | Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct | gpt2 | transfers |
|---|---|---|---|
| leading-space form is cheaper (' happened' vs 'happened') | 1 vs 3 | 1 vs 3 | yes |
| capitalisation costs tokens ('explain' vs 'Explain') | 1 vs 2 | 2 vs 2 | **NO** |
| first-word deletion can cost tokens | 11 vs 12 | 7 vs 8 | yes |
| tier-1 normalisations that save nothing | 7/20 (35%) | 7/20 (35%) | yes |

Reduction **ratios** transfer because a ratio cancels a roughly constant vocabulary factor — absolute counts differ substantially ("Please explain recursion." is 4 tokens under Qwen2.5 and 5 under GPT-2) while the percentages do not. The **mechanisms** underneath do not all transfer: GPT-2 has no capitalisation penalty, so one of ADR-030's two position-0 effects is Qwen-specific. That correction is ADR-032.

**Scope.** This is the *tokenizer* dimension of report §4.6. Decode speed, answer quality and quantisation are properties of the model, not the tokenizer, and still require a real provider.
